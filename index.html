Magnesium Glycinate Stack — Email Subject Line Sleep / recovery angle
What the category writes

LUMEN rewrite

"Waking up at 3am isn't insomnia. It's your GABA receptors — and magnesium is the one mineral that binds them."

Why it converts better: Reframes the symptom as a mechanism (GABA binding), which neutralizes the "I've tried magnesium before" objection. Specific receptor-level language signals authority without requiring a disease claim.

Testosterone Support Supplement — VSL Open High-risk claim category
What the category writes

"Clinically proven to boost testosterone levels by up to 45% in just 12 weeks."

LUMEN rewrite

"A 2021 trial in the Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition found men over 40 who took 300mg of ashwagandha daily saw measurably higher testosterone levels at 12 weeks versus placebo. Here's what that actually means for your training."

Why it converts better: Cites the real study (dose, population, timeframe, publication) instead of a vague "clinically proven" claim that invites FTC scrutiny. More specific = more credible to skeptical buyers. Ad-safe.

Compliance audit breakdown — claims that kill ad accounts and why

"Clinically proven to boost testosterone" FTC Flag "Clinically proven" requires substantiation on file. Vague "testosterone boost" without dose, population, and timeframe is an unsubstantiated structure/function claim.
"Reverses cellular aging at the DNA level" Meta Ban "Reverses aging" implies treatment of a disease process. Meta's algorithm flags "aging" + "reverses" + implied mechanism as a prohibited health claim regardless of supplement status.
"Studied in a 2020 human trial at 300mg/day" Compliant Specific citation with dose and study design is substantiatable, passes FTC structure/function rules, and does not imply disease treatment.
"Supports healthy cortisol levels already in normal range" Compliant "Already in normal range" qualifier is FTC-recommended language for structure/function claims. Avoids implied disease treatment (cortisol disorders) while remaining persuasive.
"Feel like you're 30 again" Rewrite Before/after age framing is a grey zone on Meta. Borderline-safe in body copy, high-risk in ad creative or subject lines. Reframe as mechanism or outcome specificity.
