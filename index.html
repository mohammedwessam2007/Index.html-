Compliance audit table — claims that kill ad accounts and why

These are the patterns I check for in every piece before it ships.

"Clinically proven to boost testosterone" FTC Flag "Clinically proven" requires substantiation on file. Vague "testosterone boost" without dose, population, and timeframe is an unsubstantiated structure/function claim.
"Reverses cellular aging at the DNA level" Meta Ban "Reverses aging" implies treatment of a disease process. Meta flags "aging" + "reverses" + mechanism as a prohibited health claim regardless of supplement status.
"Studied in a 2020 human trial at 300mg/day" Compliant Specific citation with dose and study design is substantiatable, passes FTC structure/function rules, and does not imply disease treatment.
"Supports healthy cortisol levels already in normal range" Compliant "Already in normal range" is FTC-recommended qualifier language for structure/function claims. Avoids implied disease treatment while remaining persuasive.
"Feel like you're 30 again" Rewrite Before/after age framing is a grey zone — safe in body copy, high-risk in ad creative or subject lines. Reframe as a mechanism or measurable outcome.
